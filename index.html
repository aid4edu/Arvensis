Our mandate in high‑stakes AI

FIVE AREAS OF WORK

When a model's output can shift public policy, redirect regulation, or touch critical infrastructure, the question is never just "does it work" — it's whether the institution around it can carry that weight.

01

Technical feasibility assessment

We evaluate whether an AI system can realistically deliver on the need in front of it — predictive policing, environmental monitoring, health diagnostics — without inheriting the vendor's optimism about what the model can actually do.

02

Innovation potential analysis

We identify where AI has genuinely transformative potential in governance, such as fraud detection in public procurement or early‑warning systems for climate risk, and where it's a well‑marketed distraction.

03

Implementation capacity review

We assess whether an agency or consortium has the institutional, data, and human capacity to deploy AI responsibly — before deployment, not after the first audit finding.

04

Regulatory & data governance oversight

We keep projects inside the EU AI Act, GDPR, and sector‑specific rules — covering risk classification, bias audits, and explainability standards — as a working discipline, not a closing checklist.

05

Knowledge transfer & policy learning

We run the workshops that connect separate projects, so governments learn from each other's pilots instead of every ministry re‑discovering the same failure mode alone.
